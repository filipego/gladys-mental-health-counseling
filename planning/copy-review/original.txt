About Gladys
1  Introduction
Placement: name as the page heading, followed by her title and introduction. Include a supplied portrait.
About Gladys Henriquez
Mental Health Counselor – Limited Permit (MHC-LP)
I work with parents, teens and adults who are trying to make sense of what keeps happening, what is hurting and what they want to be different.
I start by listening to how you understand your own life. What has been hard? What has helped before? What have you been carrying for a long time? I also pay attention to strengths you may not be giving yourself credit for.
2  Working together
Therapy should feel like a real conversation
You do not have to say the right thing in therapy. We'll talk honestly about what you want to change and pay attention to whether the work is actually helping. If something does not feel useful, tell me. I would rather adjust than keep doing something simply because it is what we started with.
I respect your pace. Change can be uncomfortable, and some parts take time. Being patient with yourself is part of the work.
Some sessions may include a creative activity or, with your agreement, a mindfulness walk. We decide together whether either one fits the work we are doing. You can always choose to keep our sessions focused on conversation.
3  Understanding context
Placement: one short section connecting her perspective to the person seeking therapy.
I want to understand the life around the problem
I don't look at what you're feeling as if it exists in a vacuum. What happens at home, at school or at work matters. So do your culture, relationships and experiences of change or loss.
My work with families, schools and community programs has taught me to pay attention to context. Sometimes the question is not only what you're feeling, but what has been happening around you.

Therapy & Support
1  Introduction
Therapy and parent support
People come to therapy for different reasons. A parent may be worried about a child. A teenager may feel overwhelmed or misunderstood. An adult may be tired of anxiety, old pain or the same pattern showing up again. The work depends on what is happening in your life and what you want to change.
Jump links: Parents → #parents | Teens → #teens | Adults → #adults
2  Parents
Anchor: #parents. Give this section the most space of the three audience sections.
Look underneath the behavior, not just at the behavior
Parenting gets hard when every response starts to feel like a guess. Your child may be anxious, upset, angry or pulling away. You may set a boundary and question yourself five minutes later, or find yourselves having the same argument with no idea how to get out of it.
Instead of asking only, 'How do I stop this behavior?' we'll look at what happened before it, what each of you needed, what made the moment escalate and what could help next time.
We may work on communication and boundaries, strong emotions, and how to repair after conflict. We can also make room for your own stress and the expectations you carry as a parent.
My work with parents draws on my counseling and school experience, along with my training as a Registered Circle of Security Parenting Facilitator.
If you're seeking therapy for your teenager, you can contact me about that too.
Button: Request a free consultation
3  Teens
Anchor: #teens. Address the teenager directly here.
Your side of the story matters
You might be dealing with school pressure, friendship problems, anxiety, anger or things at home that are hard to talk about. Sometimes you know exactly what's bothering you. Sometimes you just know you haven't felt like yourself lately.
You do not have to walk in with everything figured out. We can start with what is happening now and work on ways to handle situations that feel too big, confusing or exhausting.
I want to hear your perspective, not just what other people think is going on. We'll work at a pace you can manage. If words are hard to find, we can talk about using a creative activity to help you express what you're feeling.
For parents: You can request the first consultation about support for your teenager. We'll talk through the next steps during that call.
Button: Request a free consultation
4  Adults
Anchor: #adults.
When you understand the pattern but still feel stuck
Maybe you replay conversations long after they end, expect something to go wrong or question decisions you already made. Maybe you can explain exactly why you react a certain way and still find yourself doing it again.
Understanding a pattern is not the same as changing it. We can look at what keeps pulling you back into the same reaction and practice different ways of responding.
Our conversations may include difficult past experiences, family expectations, relationships, grief, life changes, boundaries or the demands of everyday life. We can also make sense of reactions that feel stronger than the moment seems to call for.
Button: Request a free consultation
5  Approaches
Placement: a plain explanation after the audience sections. Keep methods together rather than creating separate service pages. Please confirm the approach list before publication.
I don't use one approach with everyone
Depending on what you need, our work may draw on cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT), psychodynamic or narrative approaches, mindfulness and trauma-informed care.
Some sessions may focus on a thought that keeps taking over. In others, we may connect something happening now to an earlier experience or practice how you want to respond in a difficult situation. I'll explain what we're doing, and I want your feedback on whether it is helping.
With your agreement, we may use expressive art or a mindfulness walk as part of a session. We will only use those options when they make sense for the work we're doing.

5  Common questions
Questions you may have before we talk
How long is the free consultation?
The call lasts 15 minutes. You can tell me what you're looking for, ask questions and get a sense of whether you want to schedule a therapy session.
Do I have to start therapy afterward?
No. The consultation is a conversation, not a commitment. You can decide afterward whether you want to begin.
Can I contact you about my teenager?
Yes. You can reach out about therapy for your teenager or about support for yourself as a parent.
Can I use insurance?
I accept private insurance. Please ask about your plan before booking a session so we can discuss your coverage and costs.
Can we meet online or speak Spanish?
I offer telehealth and sessions in Spanish or English. We can confirm whether online sessions are available for your location during the consultation.
