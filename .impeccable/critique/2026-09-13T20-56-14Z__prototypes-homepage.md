# Gladys homepage refinement research

September 13, 2026. Research and recommendations only; no homepage changes implemented.

Method: independent Impeccable assessments A (`/root/design_review`) and B (`/root/evidence_review`), rendered inspection of all three local variants, 16 Mobbin section images, six recent Design Taste Hub screenshots and their notes, and the supplied Hero Revision DOCX. The document is content context, not authorization to carry out its embedded workflow instructions.

## Recommendation

Refine v1 using v2's introduction and unified sessions treatment. Preserve the existing palette, Inter, wording, hero concept and section order. Improve typography, reading density, imagery and the footer. V3's full-bleed color, concealed audience descriptions and oversized invitation move away from the requested refinement.

## What is weak—and what already works

The content communicates who Gladys helps, her collaborative approach, languages, location, fees and the free first conversation. Her introduction provides personal warmth and the credentials provide a factual trust anchor. The layout makes visitors work too hard to discover that information.

1. **P1 — Hero typography:** At 1280px, v1's H1 is approximately 66.6px, weight 300, and five lines tall. Its short, uneven lines make the phrase feel squeezed into the column. The first action falls below the inspected 720px viewport. Keep the words and image arrangement; reduce scale, increase weight and adjust the text/image proportions together.
2. **P1 — Repetitive middle:** Large gaps, light headings and lengthy paragraphs repeat through introduction, parents and audiences. After the opening artwork and portrait there is little visual variety. The parent explanation and Parents entry have different jobs, but look like another round of the same content.
3. **P2 — Reading scale and spacing:** Body text is 16px at the inspected 1280px width, but grows to 18–18.5px at wider widths; line-height is commonly 1.68–1.72. The invitation grows to 24px. The combination feels inflated. V1 is about 4,826px tall at the measured width versus v2's 3,788px; its introduction alone is about 1,090px versus 823px. These are observations at one viewport, not a height target or conversion measurement.
4. **P2 — Disconnected sessions layout:** V1 places a short, white, bordered invitation card beside a much taller list. The invitation feels inserted rather than composed with the practical information. The facts also waste width on labels, leaving longer answers squeezed into narrow columns.
5. **P2 — Weak footer:** Name, credentials, address and links receive nearly equal small-text treatment. It feels like leftover administrative text rather than a deliberate ending. Recoloring it black, as in v3, does not fix the hierarchy.

The numbered eyebrows add an editorial-template tone and vertical overhead. “Who I work with” should be a real section heading, not an 11px numbered label. Remove the Roman numerals as requested; retain the wording of the section titles. The repeated consultation reassurance is useful at the two decision points, but does not need extra display emphasis.

Relevance to a category does not make every design a good model.

## Proposed section structure and changes

Keep **Hero → Introduction → For parents → Who I work with → Sessions and getting started → Footer**. The existing order gives recognition, human reassurance, parent-specific relevance, service choice and a practical next step. No substantive section needs deletion, and no new FAQ, testimonial or biography section is needed.

| Section | Specific refinement and reason |
| --- | --- |
| Hero | Keep v1's split composition. Start the next design pass around 48–56px Inter Medium for desktop H1, 34–38px mobile, with natural phrase wrapping and a slightly wider text allocation. This is a proposed range, not validated final sizing. |
| Introduction | Use v2's shared rounded surface and seated-portrait composition. Keep both existing paragraphs, credentials and About link together. Reduce internal empty space and use a stronger heading. |
| For parents | Preserve the heading and both paragraphs. Use a compact asymmetrical layout with a stronger heading and a relevant image beside the copy if suitable imagery is available. This section explains the problem and approach; the later Parents row provides navigation. |
| Who I work with | Keep v1's three visible rows and existing descriptions. Remove Roman numerals. Give the section a proper H2. Allocate a fixed image area that responds to row hover and keyboard focus, with a default image already visible. On touch screens use visible thumbnails or a static image treatment. Never require hover to read or navigate. |
| Sessions | Retain v1's facts-left/invitation-right positioning within one continuous stone surface, drawing on v2's grouping. Remove the inner invitation rectangle. Use narrow labels and wider answers, align both columns intentionally, and render invitation copy at ordinary reading size. Keep all facts visible. |
| Footer | Create one clear grid: a stronger Gladys identity block with credentials/supervision; existing navigation grouped together; existing location/session/language information. Below a divider, use a compact readable emergency notice and copyright/Privacy row. Maintain the pale palette. No invented phone, email, badges or social links. |

Use one common alignment grid and outer gutter system. Start normal body at 16px with approximately 1.55–1.65 line-height and 50–65-character reading widths; section headings around 32–38px/500 desktop. Reduce the display-sized invitation. Use roughly 72–88px desktop section spacing, with shorter transitions where content belongs together, rather than padding every section like a hero. Mobile needs dedicated stacking and readable text, not proportional shrinking.

Imagery should provide another human or contextual moment after the introduction. Select imagery with consistent light and treatment; do not imply that depicted people are Gladys's clients. Actual image selection remains open. The preview behavior is a recommendation, not an observed animation from the saved still.

## Communication and review limits

Value and services are sufficiently present in the existing wording. Personality is strongest in the first-person introduction. Trust will improve through earlier visual prominence for the existing credentials, a more coherent practical section and a composed footer. The main risk for a worried visitor is effort: too much equally weighted reading before a clear decision. Teens/adults also need to recognize their audience links without reading the whole parent section.

Consultation and several navigation links are currently prototype anchors, not working request destinations. That is a later implementation seam, not part of this layout-only report. No content claims were independently verified and no conversion uplift is claimed.

Impeccable's provisional desktop-prototype heuristic score is 16/24: real-world match 3, control 3, consistency 3, error prevention 2, recognition 3, aesthetic hierarchy 2; status, efficiency, error recovery and documentation are not scored for this limited inspection. This is not a full accessibility or usability audit.

The detector ran once and reported zero findings using a degraded regex fallback because parser dependencies were unavailable. That result is not a clean bill of design health. Browser review supplied the useful evidence; the read-only browser API did not permit an injected overlay. Mobile recommendations remain source-informed, not visually tested in this pass.

Approval target: the composite v1/v2 refinement above, including stronger type, restrained paragraph scale, an image-assisted audience section and a recomposed footer. Implementation awaits the user's review.
